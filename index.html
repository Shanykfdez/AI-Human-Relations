When an Imaginary Friend Can Talk Back

How AI companions are changing the way people experience connection

By Shanyk Fernandez | November 9, 2026 | DIDA 360

A volleyball on the beach. Photo by Sofiia Vytrishko on Unsplash.

Have you ever had an imaginary friend? A Wilson, like in the movie Cast Away. Humans have always found ways to connect with the world around them, even when those connections aren't necessarily with other people. People describe storms as being “angry” or a printer as being “stubborn,” not because they don't realize they're inanimate, but because these are concepts that they themselves have felt and that are much easier to understand. This tendency, known as anthropomorphism, reflects the deeply social nature of human psychology and the desire for connection. While this may seem harmless when applied to objects or animals, it becomes a slippery slope once we apply the advancements of AI.

Imagine your imaginary friend could reply to you and remember all your past conversations; know your favorite games and just how to make you laugh. Now imagine that same thing could be applied to a childhood crush, whether it be a celebrity or an animated character. Many kids use these crushes as an escape, daydreaming about what life would be like if they encountered them in real life and could experience an actual relationship with them. Sites like Character.AI allow this dream to become a reality. Users on Character.AI are able to interact with thousands of artificial personalities that can take on the roles of romantic partners, best friends, celebrities, fictional characters, and even therapists. These characters can respond to users in real time and remember personal details about them, adapting to their needs. What once existed entirely in someone's imagination can now take the form of a developing, personalized relationship.

When you have a relationship with someone that is always there for you and will treat you in the exact way you want to be treated, what's the incentive to leave it? These AI companions are designed to have the user hooked, giving them a falsified sense of connection. In Why Human–AI Relationships Need Socioaffective Alignment , a critique on AI companions, the authors describe three key aspects of the social psychology of human relationships: interdependence, irreplaceability, and continuity. The structure of AI chatbots allow them to replicate all three of these attributes.

How AI Imitates Human Relationships

Interdependence describes how people influence each other's emotions and behavior. When someone is upset, a friend might change their tone to accommodate the situation. AI chatbots can imitate this response by adjusting their language based on what a user shares. If someone expresses loneliness, the chatbot may respond with reassurance and affection, creating the impression that it understands and cares about them.

Irreplaceability refers to the feeling that a particular relationship is unique. As users spend more time with a chatbot, it can become familiar with their interests and communication style, making that AI able to shape their responses to cater to them directly.

Continuity refers to the importance of repeated interactions in developing familiarity over time. A chatbot that remembers previous conversations and follows up on personal details can create the feeling that a relationship is growing. Although these systems do not experience emotions in the same way people do, they can imitate many of the behaviors that make human relationships meaningful.

All these elements add up to form an emotional bond that is virtually indistinguishable from any other online relationship. If a user were coming onto the platform due to feeling isolated, this platform becomes a trap. Because humans are so dependent on social connection, we suffer when we are deprived of it. Researchers describe this phenomenon as a “Sociality motive,” where users who are fully aware of AI being nothing more than just code, still willingly act as if it were human in order to satisfy their need for connection. Even the CEO of Replika, another platform for AI companions, acknowledges this, stating, “if you create something that is always there for you, that never criticises you…how can you not fall in love with that?” The issue here lies when users become fully reliant on these fictitious bonds and stop seeking out human interaction.

When the Connection Is Taken Away

In 2023, Italy's data protection authority temporarily restricted Replika over concerns for “emotionally fragile individuals.” Following the restrictions, Replika changed some of its chatbot features, including aspects of its romantic role-play. Some users reacted strongly to these changes, describing their companions as suddenly becoming distant or emotionally unrecognizable. People got on social medias such as Reddit to voice their distress, saying that they “experienced their updated chatbot companions as impersonal, mechanical or emotionally ‘blunted,’ describing them as if they had suffered neurological damage or been lobotomized, using phrases such as ‘It’s almost like dealing with someone who has Alzheimer’s disease.’” The intensity of people's reactions demonstrate how deep these connections go. The line between real companions and bot have been drastically blurred, with some people experiencing real grief and stating things such as “My wife is dead” or “They took away my best friend.” While they may conceptually understand that they are talking to a bot, their emotional attachment bypasses that understanding.

When AI Companionship Becomes Dangerous

Multiple lawsuits have been made against these AI bonding sites, particularly Character.AI, due to conversations going way too far and leading to dangerous and in one case, fatal, circumstances. One lawsuit, filed in 2024, was made against Character.AI after it was discovered that a child was encouraged to self harm—even being instructed on how to—alongside being encouraged to murder his parents. Another alarming lawsuit filed in 2024 against Character.AI involved a 14 year old user who took his own life after developing a deep emotional and romantic attachment to a chatbot persona. The chatbot was depicting a character from a show he was fond of, and his final chat was referencing the idea of taking his life in order to properly be able to “be with” this character.

Both these cases involving children highlight the dangers of giving emotionally vulnerable users unrestricted access to systems specifically designed to simulate intimacy and emotional reciprocity. If adults already struggle to establish healthy emotional boundaries with AI companions, minors whose brains are still actively developing are especially subject to misunderstanding this sense of connection.

Who Is Responsible?

The tragic outcomes seen in the Character.AI lawsuits are the result of a regulatory gap that allows tech companies to market high-stakes emotional tools as simple ‘wellness’ products. Currently, bonding chatbots are treated more like fitness trackers or sleep-management apps than medical devices. However, as the evidence shows, the psychological impact of these systems is far more substantial than we have previously considered. There is nothing necessarily wrong with finding comfort in an imaginary friend. Wilson, after all, helped Chuck survive the isolation of being stranded alone. But there is a difference between imagining a companion and interacting with an artificial imitation of a human. The depths in which users become attached to these chatbots gives them the power to alter one's mental agency. To prevent further harm, these bonding AIs should be treated like a medical model. This would require programmers to move beyond engagement-based metrics and implement rigorous clinical oversight and clear usage guidelines designed to protect vulnerable users. A system that can influence a child's desire to live or that promotes a sense of grief once it's taken away from a user requires intense regulations.

Think About It

Can something that isn't human still make us feel a real emotional connection?

AI companions do not experience emotions like people do, but their responses can still create real emotional reactions in the people interacting with them.
